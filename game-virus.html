What it is

BandiThe whole game is a desktop. Folders, a dock, a trash can. A timer in the corner counts down from 300 seconds until tech support arrives. Meanwhile a virus is working through your files, turning them red one by one, and pop-ups keep opening: install this antivirus, find your dream house, free game codes.▼
BandiThe folder that matters is locked, and the hint is a riddle: your favourite film, your favourite game, your cat's birthday. The answers are scattered through the other files, a to-do list, a photo, a birthday note. Open the right ones, work out the password, then upload the folder to the drive and keep that window open until it finishes. The virus doesn't wait.

What I made

BandiAll of it. The desktop itself, the window system, the folders and files, the virus that corrupts them one by one, the antivirus app that only tells you how bad it is, the upload bar, the pop-ups and the timing of when they show up. And the pop-up art, which is supposed to look like it was drawn by someone who really wants you to click.
BandiIt's the videogame version of my board game of the same name, but this time you're alone and the virus doesn't take turns.

What I learned

BandiThis was my first time using Unity. Before this I had only made things in Snap! and Ren'Py, where the engine does most of the work for you. Here I had to learn how the engine actually thinks: scenes and game objects, components and scripts in C#, how a timer or a pop-up is really just an object that someone has to tell when to appear.
BandiSo the thing I learned is less "how to make this game" and more "how to make Unity do what I want." That part carries over to whatever I build next.
