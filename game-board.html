Game goals

virus

Seize 3 files before tiles or pieces run out.

PC

Stop the virus from seizing files until all tiles have been placed, or kill all virus pieces.

What it is

BandiThe board is a motherboard. The four files in the corners are what the PC is trying to protect. Both players start in the centre block. At first the virus can only travel on red wires and the PC only on blue; purple connects to either. Once either side seizes a file, both can travel on any wire.▼
BandiYou build the circuit as you play: place a tile, every wire has to meet a wire of a matching colour. Then move. Seize a file and you get another piece. Fully block someone's path and their piece dies. It's the tabletop version of the videogame of the same name, except here the virus is a person across the table.

Game assets

How to play

Rules

1

Place tiles on a 9×9 board, starting from the centre block.

2

The virus side goes first; each player may place at most 1 tile per turn.

3

Each player gets one game piece at the start, on the centre block.

4

Virus travels only on red wires at the start, PC only on blue. Once either seizes a block, both can travel on any wire.

5

Players cannot pass directly through each other's pieces.

6

Every time a player seizes a file, they get one more piece in the centre block.

7

Players can kill each other's pieces by fully blocking their path.

8

60 cards total. If all are placed and the game isn't over, whoever has seized the most files wins.

On the table

The tiles

BandiThe road tokens, drawn one by one in Magma. Straight red, straight blue, purple crossings, corners, end nodes, turn nodes. The whole game is which of these you draw and where you're willing to put them.

What I learned

BandiWhat a board game is, and isn't. Compared with a video game there is much less room for story and for time: no cutscenes, no clock running in the background, nothing happens unless a hand moves it.
BandiBut the rules are looser in a way code can never be. Players bend them, agree on edge cases, house-rule the parts that don't work, and the game survives. A program crashes when a rule is unclear; two people at a table just talk.
